I was recently asked... "when developing or improving software, how do you determine which technical improvements will have the most meaningful impact on the actual users?"

two ways customers tell you what to build:

**Indirectly, through friction.** Support tickets about "inconsistent" numbers were customers telling you something, just not in words. The real problem wasn't the math. The same metric was shown to five different roles with different scopes and timing, and nobody had written down what each number meant. You'd shipped the feature and moved on, so even the team had to reverse-engineer its own definitions.

**Directly**, by asking. The Head Start workshop: you went to them, listened in small groups, and turned what you heard into epics.

Most of the time, what we built came down from Product. The best decisions came from getting closer to the customer, either by reading the signals they were already sending or by sitting in the room with them.

## Situation

HeadStart was nearly half of our revenue stream. To better serve our customer we decided to hold a workshop at one of their regional conferences and meet with our customers directly.

## Task

- Collaborate with Product to structure and organize a workshop
- Gain insight to customer needs/wants directly from the customer
- Gain brand visibility, loyalty, and credibility

## Action

- Collaboratively planned workshop event and ways to extract user feedback
- Held workshop using small group format led by team member
- Interviewed our respective groups and listened to their wants/needs
- Debriefed with the team
- Worked with Product to help create roadmap and help write Epic requirements
- Prioritized and performed Epic and related stories

## Result

- We extracted two significant feature needs to directly serve the customer
- We satisfied one of those needs, the other one was a trade off to the product roadmap
- We gained customer satisfaction, credibility, brand loyalty, as well as more robust product

ok, I have a start on the article. I haven't written the outline yet but will shortly. I have the page, and an image. I also have my STAR story (pasted) and what's left of my memory. Missing will likely be my knowledge on how most companies, or even ours, determine what to build. More often than not, that decision came from upstream by way of Product. Sometimes we'd create an ad-hock team to go over some feature ideas or requests that were coming in from the field, or.. we were getting a lot of support inquiries over what was being perceived as data inconsistencies between data points. The problem was once we shipped the data points, we'd moved on to the next feature. So, for us, we had to stop and re-learn what a particular data point meant in one place, then in the other, then figure out why they weren't resulting in the same numbers. More often than not, it wasn't intended to be the same number. In fact, the number was being presented to two or three different Roles (Org Admin, District Admin, School Admin, Teacher, Caregiver), so they weren't supposed to be the same number. And if you added them all up they still would be the same number because of reporting timing or something like that. Sometimes there was a disrepancy due to scoping issues... a Teacher would belong to two different districts or something. To help minimize the support requests I suggested being forthcoming in the data point definitions. The best way to do that was a tool tip responding to a hover action. If need be, a "learn more" link could be inserted that could take them to a data point definitions page. We implemented the tool tip and that's as far as we got. The Headstart workshop was a much more rewarding experience though.

Try to recall specifics, even rough ones:

- What were the two features, in plain terms?
- Why was the second one traded off? Too big, off-strategy, or bad timing?
- What did customers say or do afterward that showed it mattered?
- Did the tooltip actually reduce support tickets, even anecdotally?

Keep in mind that the data point story fits Ignite especially well. They send progress data to districts, teachers, and families, which is the same multi-role reporting problem you've already dealt with. That's a nice thread to pick up in outreach later.
